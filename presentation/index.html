PROJECT STORY · RYAN LI · OCTOBER 2026

Tennis101

A tennis game where you play inside a real pro's body. Height, weight and reach change how you move, serve and tire.

Above: Jannik Sinner's real first serve, captured from video, cleaned, and replayed as joint dots. The same data now drives him in the game.

01 · THE PROBLEM

In most tennis games, every pro plays the same.

Animation lock

Press a button and the game carries you to the ball. Footwork doesn't matter.

Same body

A 1.91 m player and a 1.83 m player move identically. Only the ratings change.

Free stamina

Energy refills every point, so a 4-hour match feels like the first game.

My rule: every physical trait is a trade-off you can feel, never just a bigger number.

03 · BODIES THAT OBEY PHYSICS

Height and weight become movement

SinnerAlcaraz
Height · weight1.91 m · 77 kg1.83 m · 74 kg
Top speed5.35 m/s5.29 m/s
First step6.7 m/s²7.1 m/s²
Braking8.8 m/s²9.1 m/s²
Reach1.53 m1.50 m

Longer legs → longer stride → higher top speed. More mass → slower to start and stop.

What the player feels

  • Momentum: to change direction you must brake first, so you can be wrong-footed.
  • Split-step: land it within ±0.07 s of the opponent's hit for a 1.8× first step.
  • Stamina: only 35% comes back between points; the ceiling drops all match.

Contracts

Agents agree on interfaces (launchBall, getPose, SERVE_MOTIONS) instead of editing each other's code.

Memory

Each run starts fresh, so every agent keeps a log in its folder. The log is how it knows where it stopped.

Version history

Small changes wait in contemporary/; five of them become one version with a playable copy.

07 · THE BUG I CAUGHT

At the toss, the skeleton fell apart

Raw, frame 60: neck and shoulders jump onto the raised hand, so the torso stretches up to the ball.

Cleaned: the torso is the right length and the tossing arm on top reaches up. Hollow dots were rebuilt.

My fix: when the arms fold or overlap, whichever arm is on top is the one that's shown.

11 · WHAT I LEARNED

What I learned, and what's next

Learned

  • Look at the data before trusting it: raw pose looked fine until I watched frame 60.
  • Rules beat guesses: one clear rule (arm on top is shown) fixed most frames.
  • Parallel work needs contracts and ownership, or agents overwrite each other.
  • Store data so you can ask questions of it: SQL turned a hunch into a number.

Next

  • Serve control: toss height, power and aim in one motion; sweet spot; spin.
  • Real ball physics with surface-specific bounces.
  • More pros, each with their own captured serve.
  • First-person camera at the pro's real eye height.
